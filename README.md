# Churn-analysis
An interactive business intelligence dashboard designed to analyze customer churn, identify revenue at risk, and support data-driven retention strategies. This project provides clear visual insights into customer behavior, churn risk levels, payment methods, internet services, contract terms, tenure, and monthly charges to help organizations improve customer retention and reduce revenue loss.

## Dashboard Insights
- The dashboard analyzes 7,043 customers with a 27% churn rate and total revenue of 456.12K, where 31% revenue is at risk.
- Customers with high monthly charges recorded the highest churn at about 2.6K customers.
- Electronic check users showed the highest churn among payment methods with nearly 2.4K churn cases.
- Fiber optic customers had the highest internet service churn, exceeding 3K customers.
- Customers with 0–2 years tenure contributed the most churn at approximately 3.2K customers.
- Month-to-month contracts experienced the highest churn with nearly 3.8K customers, compared to lower churn in one- and two-year contracts.
- Risk segmentation shows 37.75% low-risk, 36.31% medium-risk, and 25.94% high-risk customers.

## Recommendations
- Focus retention campaigns on the 27% churn population, especially customers contributing to the 31% revenue at risk.
- Introduce loyalty discounts or flexible pricing for customers with high monthly charges, since they account for approximately 2.6K churn cases.
- Improve customer experience and billing support for electronic check users, as they represent the highest payment-method churn at nearly 2.4K customers.
- Prioritize service quality improvements for fiber optic customers, where churn exceeds 3K customers, by reducing outages and improving support response times.
- Develop onboarding and engagement programs for customers within their first 0–2 years, since this group contributes about 3.2K churned customers.
- Encourage customers to move from month-to-month contracts to longer-term plans using discounts, rewards, or bundled services, as month-to-month contracts show nearly 3.8K churn cases.
